# Your profile: the eight things the agent needs to know about your business

**This file is the only place your company's facts go.** Not the job description, not the playbooks. Those two files are the same for everybody; this one is yours, and it is why the agent can be handed to someone else.

Fill it with your own answers. The agent reads it, with the binder files beside it, at the start of every run and never edits either.

Mark every line one of three ways: **found** with a source, **inferred**, or **TO CONFIRM**. A profile full of honest TO CONFIRMs is useful. A profile of confident guesses is worse than an empty one, because you will not remember which lines you made up.

---

## 1. What you sell, and how you say it
- The company, in one line.
- Your lines of business.
- The sentence you actually use to describe it. In your words, not marketing's.
- **The words you do not use.** This matters more than it sounds. List the terms your business exists against, the ones a competitor would use. The agent will avoid them, and a card that uses one sounds like it came from someone else.

## 2. Your own domains
- Every domain you own, so the agent can tell an internal meeting from an external one.
- **Your partners' domains, listed separately and clearly marked as not yours.** A meeting with a partner is external. Getting this wrong silently drops real meetings, and you will not notice until you miss one.

## 3. Who is worth your time
- The shape that fits: company type, size, geography, the team being served.
- The buying trigger.
- **Who does not fit, and when you say no.** Usually the most useful field here and usually the emptiest.
- **The disqualifier the agent should watch for.** One thing that, if true, means the meeting is not what it looks like.

## 4. The committee: who signs, who champions, who just attends
The field that makes this agent work, and the one to spend real time on.

For each seat, the **titles you actually see** in your own deals:
- **economic buyer**: signs
- **champion**: feels the pain, brings you in, defends the spend
- **user**: sits in the room, never signs
- **blocker**: can stop it, and what they say when they do

Then: **at what headcount does the answer change?** A Head of Sales signs at 20 people and does not at 2000. The agent takes size as an input, so it needs your threshold, not a generic one.

## 5. What counts as now
- **Tier 1, act now:** the specific, checkable signal that means this account is live. Narrow beats broad. "Posted about hiring SDRs" is a signal. "Shows buying intent" is not.
- **Tier 2, worth a line.**
- **Tier 3, context only, never a trigger.**
- **Competitor and vendor domains**, so the agent does not read a competitor's own marketing as your prospect's buying signal.

## 6. Who you can name
- Every customer you may say out loud, with who said it, their industry, and the line. **Public only.**
- The numbers you can cite, each with its source.
- **Which customers are confidential.** The agent names nobody outside the permitted list, so anything you leave off is automatically safe. Leaving a name off costs you one proof point. Putting a confidential one on costs you a customer.

## 7. The seats you sell to
One short file per seat: what they are measured on, their biggest fear, what they do not care about, what they say before they buy, and the proof that moves them.

## 8. What you actually solve
One short file per problem: who feels it, what it looks like, why they have not fixed it, your counter, and the receipt.

---

## The two fields nobody fills, and both are the valuable ones
**"Who does not fit"** and **"which customers are confidential."** Both are about absence, so neither has a web page to copy from, and both can only come from you.

Everything else in this profile can be drafted from your website and corrected in twenty minutes. These two cannot be drafted at all.
